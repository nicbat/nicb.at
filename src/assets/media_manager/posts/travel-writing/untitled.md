my essay from travel writing on that time (I would want to rewrite it):\
After our trip to the Basilica, the 3 of us squished into a small, air-conditioned taxi on our way to Hard Rock Cafe. Even though we felt cramped, the gentle breeze of the car fan relieved us from the sweaty hours of our early morning class. We ordered food and listened to the ambient American rock music playing in the background.

Sitting in this restaurant brought back memories of my punk rock best friend from middle school, helping me feel a little closer to home. My apple juice, hot herb fries, and Amatriciana Burger came out of the oven quickly, breaking our long-lasting streak of solely Italian food.

Energized by food reminiscent of home, we felt ready to set out to the Vatican by my favorite form of transportation – electric scooter! Our GPS took us up the hill, through the Villa Borghese, and then straight to the Vatican. Much like previous scooter rides in Rome, our high speeds kept us cool as we zoomed through flocks of tourists.

However, our scooter fun ended quickly – my thumb started bleeding, my phone wouldn't stay in place, and it cracked on the dirty cobblestones. As we looked for the main entrance to the Vatican, we slowed down and the bright sun's heat made me feel like I was melting under my "formal" Vatican-ready clothing. When we parked our scooters, I checked my tour reservation and learned that we wouldn't be able to enter the Vatican for another couple hours. Overwhelmed by frustration, we sat down in the overcrowded and overpriced Caffe Vaticano, sure to get our money's worth.

Two hours and forty euro later, we left the crowds of tourists and red-checkered tablecloths and made our way to the tour office down the street. Happy we were out of the tourist trap, we all laughed about Manav's 15 euro tiny fruit plate while sitting in little floor chairs in the tour office. Its walls were covered with little sharpie drawings by tourists from all over the world, and the owners gave us Italian music recommendations.

Looking back, I wish we were able to spend more time in those really comfy chairs, but sadly, we headed out at around 6pm to the Vatican Museums. We met someone named Danny who works for the Guardian, and we were able to skip the line and head straight into the museums. Shortly after leaving our temporary new friend, we lost ourselves in the mind-blowing assortment of artifacts that the Vatican "collected", with the ancient map room being my favorite.

After moving from museum to museum and following signs pointing to the Sistine Chapel for over 20 minutes, we made it inside to the largest room yet. The magnitude of the chapel was shocking to say the least, but honestly, I felt overstimulated by the hundreds if not thousands of ancient artifacts I spent hours perusing.

Leaving the museum brought us out into the cool night of the city, and once again we were greeted by an air-conditioned taxicab as we headed back to our apartment for the night.
